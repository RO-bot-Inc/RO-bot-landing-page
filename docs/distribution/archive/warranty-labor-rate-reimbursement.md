```
The factory pays your warranty labor at one rate. You charge cash customers a higher one.

Most states now say the manufacturer has to close that gap and reimburse you at or near your retail rate. Plenty of dealers still get paid below it.

The reason is not the law. The law does not raise your rate on its own. You have to file for it, and the filing runs on a sample of your customer-pay repair orders, usually around 100 of them.

Here is the part that decides whether it sticks. That sample only proves a rate as high as your documentation supports. Thin ROs, missing time punches, vague stories, and the manufacturer has room to dispute the number and chip it back down.

So the same documentation that gets a warranty claim paid also sets the rate that claim pays at. Two stores with the identical sign out front can submit very different rates, depending on what actually lands on the ticket.

Wrote up how the submission works, and why your repair orders decide the outcome.

Full post in the comments.
```

**Body:**

```
Quick one this morning.

The factory reimburses your warranty labor below what you charge a cash customer. Most states now require them to close that gap and pay at or near your retail rate. The money is real: one industry worked example put a single store at roughly $222,000 a year in lost gross from a below-retail rate.

The law does not hand you the rate, though. You file for it, and the filing runs on a sample of your customer-pay repair orders, commonly around 100 of them. The manufacturer calculates your retail rate from that sample.

That is where most submissions live or die. The sample only proves a rate as high as your documentation supports. Missing time punches, missing signatures, and vague stories give the manufacturer room to dispute the number. The same clean repair orders that get a claim paid are what hold the rate once you win it.

The new post walks through how the submission works and what protects it.

Read it: https://tenthgear.ai/blog/warranty-labor-rate-reimbursement
```
