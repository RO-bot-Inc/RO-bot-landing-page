The problem

Your techs fix the car. Then they have to write about it.

A warranty story is the tech's proof that the repair was needed and done right. Most techs would rather do another job than type one, so stories come out thin: "pump leaking, replaced pump."

Thin stories get kicked back. Somebody rewrites them, the claim waits, and sometimes the dealership eats the labor.

Questions

FAQs

What is a 3C story?
A 3C story, also called the 3Cs, is the write-up a technician adds to each repair line: the concern (what the customer reported, also called the complaint), the cause (what the tech found and how they confirmed it) and the correction (what was repaired or replaced, and how the fix was verified). Warranty administrators submit it with the claim, and the manufacturer uses it to decide whether to pay.
Complaint or concern: which is it?
Both mean the same thing. Some automakers say complaint, some say concern. TenthGear labels the sections complaint, cause and correction.
What does a good 3C story look like?

Complaint (concern): Customer states coolant smell after driving.

Cause: Verified concern. Coolant low in reservoir. Pressure tested cooling system at 16 psi and found the water pump dripping from the weep hole, with coolant trailing down the block. Failed water pump seal.

Correction: Replaced water pump and gasket. Refilled and bled cooling system. System holds 16 psi. Road tested, no leaks.

What does a warranty reviewer look for in a 3C story?
A reviewer wants to see that the concern was verified, how it was diagnosed, the specific part, the root cause, the repair, and proof the repair worked. For diagnostic warranty work, TenthGear's grader checks for exactly those six things.
Does the AI make anything up?
It writes only from the tech's notes and the complaint already on the RO. Each line can be set to one of three modes: Strict states only what the tech said, Standard (the default) adds professional phrasing without adding claims, and Enhanced writes out the steps behind what the tech said, within strict bounds, and never adds a test, reading or part they didn't mention. Guide me never guesses a fault code, reading or part number. Every section stays editable before it's copied.
How is a story graded?
Each criterion gets a letter grade, and the story gets an overall grade. A missing item shows as a dash with a suggestion for what to add. The tech adds it, taps Re-Grade, and the grade updates.
What is Guide me?
On a phone, a diagnostic warranty line has a Guide me button beside Write Story. It checks the notes first, then asks one short question at a time about what's missing, with answers to tap, or the tech can say or type their own. Each answer is saved as a story note, and when the six warranty criteria are covered, Write Story runs as usual.
How much time does it save?
AI Story Writer alone saves about 7 minutes per repair order. Pilot shops report saving 30+ minutes a day on story writing.
Does it work in a loud shop?
The tech holds the mic button and talks with the phone close, the way they would on a call. Every note shows on the line before the story is written, so a misheard word gets fixed in seconds, and every section stays editable.
Does it write customer-pay stories too?
Yes. Customer-pay stories are graded on five criteria, including plain language and a professional tone, because the customer reads them. Warranty scheduled maintenance lines are graded on four.
Does it work with our DMS?
Yes. Each story section has a Copy button, so the tech or the warranty admin pastes complaint, cause and correction straight into the DMS. On Reynolds, turn on blue-screen formatting in your profile and each section gets Copy to CC Screen and Copy to M# Screen buttons. TenthGear currently integrates with Auto/Mate, Tekion, CDK, Reynolds, Dealertrack, PBS, Quorum, Autosoft, DealerBuilt and Dominion, and DMS integration isn't required for all implementations.
We run several stores. Does it work across all of them?
Yes. One login covers all your stores, and you switch between them without logging out.
Why not just use ChatGPT?
A chatbot writes from whatever is pasted in and doesn't know what a warranty reviewer checks. TenthGear writes from the tech's notes and the complaint on the RO, never adds a repair the tech didn't describe, and grades the story on six warranty criteria before it goes on the claim.
