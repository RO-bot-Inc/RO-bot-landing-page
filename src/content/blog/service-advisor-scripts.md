The service advisor scripts that work best don't sound like sales. They explain what the technician found, show it, give the customer a clear choice with a price, and make declining easy. Customers approve more work when they trust the finding, understand the risk of waiting, and don't feel pressured. Below are scripts for the most common moments, plus the habits that make them work.

## Four principles behind every good script

1. **Lead with what was found, not what you want to sell.** "Your technician measured your front pads at 2 millimeters" is a fact. "You need brakes" is a pitch.
2. **Show it.** A photo or short video of the actual condition does more than any wording. See [What Customers Actually Want to See in an Inspection Video](/blog/what-customers-want-inspection-videos/).
3. **Prioritize.** Separate safety items, items that should be done soon, and items to watch.
4. **Make "no" easy.** Customers who feel they can decline are more likely to say yes, and more likely to come back.

## Script 1: Calling with inspection results

*Prices in these scripts are examples. Use your store's menu pricing.*

"Hi Maria, this is James at the service department. Your car's in good shape overall. Your technician did a full inspection and found two things I want to go over. I just texted you a short video so you can see what he saw. Do you have a minute now, or would later be better?"

Then: "First, the front brake pads are at 2 millimeters. New pads are around 10, and we recommend replacing them at 4. That's a safety item. Replacing the front pads and rotors is $480, and we can have it done by 4 today. Second, your cabin air filter is dirty. That one isn't urgent; it's $65 if you'd like it done while the car is here. Which of these would you like us to take care of?"

## Script 2: Customer says "I need to think about it"

"That makes sense. I'll note it on your record so we can check it next visit. Just so you know, the brakes are already below where we'd recommend replacing them, so I'd plan on doing them in the next few weeks. Would it help if I sent you the quote and the video so you have them?"

## Script 3: Customer says it's too expensive

"I understand. If it helps, the brakes are the priority. The other item can wait. Would you like to do just the brakes today and we'll keep the rest on your record?"

## Script 4: Customer questions whether the work is needed

"Good question. Here's the measurement your technician took, and here's the video. We recommend replacing pads at 4 millimeters, and yours are at 2. If you'd like, you're welcome to come back and look at it with the technician before you decide."

## Script 5: Following up on declined work

"Hi Maria, it's James from the service department. When you were in last month, your technician found the front brakes were getting low. I wanted to check in and see if you'd like to get that scheduled. We have openings Thursday morning."

See [The Cheapest Revenue in Your Store Is Sitting in Declined Work](/blog/recover-declined-service-work/) for how to build a follow-up process.

## Script 6: Text message with inspection results

"Hi Maria, your inspection is done. Your technician recorded a short video of two items: front brakes (recommended today) and cabin filter (optional). Tap the link below to see the video and approve. Questions? Just reply to this text. James"

## What makes scripts fail

- **No evidence.** Without photos, video, or measurements, the advisor is asking the customer to take their word for it.
- **Every item marked urgent.** Customers stop believing the urgency.
- **Vague findings from the bay.** An advisor can only explain what the technician recorded. "Brakes low" gives them nothing to work with.
- **Slow callbacks.** The longer the customer waits, the more likely they decline.
