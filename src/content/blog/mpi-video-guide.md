Your store tracks video percentage. Your techs know it. So a lot of MPI videos look like this: 30 seconds of a tech circling the car and ducking under the lift so the video counts, then straight back to the job that pays.

That video does not sell anything. A good MPI video does, and the difference is not camera skill. It is a shot list.

> **TL;DR:** A good MPI video follows the same order every time: introduce yourself, answer the customer's concern, show red items, then yellow, and close with what is urgent and what can wait. One to two minutes on a clean car. Video what moves or makes noise; leave the routine measurements to the report.

## What is an MPI video?

An MPI video is a recording the technician makes during the multi-point inspection (MPI) and the advisor sends to the customer with the estimate. It is not a single clip of one worn part. It is the whole inspection, start to finish, in the tech's own voice: the lines on the RO and every item flagged red or yellow.

If you want the talk track for one concern (problem, explanation, comparison, consequence, recommendation), we covered that in [what customers actually want to see in an inspection video](/blog/what-customers-want-inspection-videos/). This post is about the full MPI video: the order, the length, and what to leave out.

In the same study, **49%** of consumers said seeing photos or videos makes them more likely to approve recommended work.

That is a correlation, not proof that the video caused the spend. But it matches what the techs who do videos well say. One 30-year dealership master tech, in a [thread full of techs complaining about MPI videos](https://www.reddit.com/r/Justrolledintotheshop/comments/1c1kinm/video_inspections/), put it this way: "Everyone including me bitched about not getting paid for it... we sell way more service and repair work than we ever did before."

## Why techs hate MPI videos (and they have a point)

Read that same thread and three complaints come up again and again.

1. **It is unpaid flat-rate time.** "How can these videos be SO critical and yet not worth paying us ANYTHING for doing them?" the original poster asked. Another tech: "I would have zero complaints if we got one or two tenths for the videos."
2. **Not every tech wants to be on camera.** "I went to school and trained to be a technician, not a damn TikToker."
3. **Forced videos get faked.** When video percentage is the metric, techs hit the metric. One described their videos as "30 seconds of me running around and under the car."

None of that is fixed by telling techs to try harder. It is fixed by making the video faster to shoot, easier to talk through, and worth something to the tech.

## The MPI video shot list

Same order on every car. When the order never changes, the tech stops thinking about what comes next and the customer learns where to look.

| # | Shot | What to say | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intro | Your name and the customer's concern in their words. "Hi Sarah, I'm Mike, I'm working on your Tahoe today for the clunk over bumps." | 10 sec |
| 2 | The concern | What you found on the line they came in for. Start wide so they know where on the car you are, then move in. | 20 to 40 sec |
| 3 | Red items | Anything that should be fixed today, one at a time. Show it, give the measurement if there is one, say why it matters. | 20 to 30 sec each |
| 4 | Yellow items | What is wearing and roughly when it will need attention. | 15 sec each |
| 5 | Close | What is urgent, what can wait, and that the advisor will follow up with pricing. | 10 sec |

Notice what is not on the list: a tour of every green item, or a reading of all four tires and brakes. Measurements belong on the report. Say one on camera only when it is the reason for a red or yellow item ("front pads are at 3 millimeters"). One sentence covers the greens ("everything else we checked is in good shape"), and the customer report shows the rest. For the full list of items and where red and yellow start, see our [multi-point inspection checklist](/blog/multi-point-inspection-checklist/).

### Video it or just say it?

Video is slow to shoot and slow to watch, so spend it where a photo or a number on the report cannot do the job.

| Video it | Report it (a measurement or photo on the report is enough) |
|---|---|
| Play in a ball joint or tie rod end | Tire tread depth |
| A ticking, grinding or squealing noise | Brake pad thickness |
| An engine jumping on a bad motor mount | Battery test result |
| A leak that is actively dripping | Dirty cabin or engine air filter |
| A wobble with the wheel in the air | Wiper blades, bulbs |

## What to leave out of an MPI video

- **Prices.** That is the advisor's conversation, with the full estimate in front of them. A tech quoting a number on camera sets a price the advisor has to defend.
- **Shop noise and shop talk.** Customers hear everything. One advisor on Reddit described a walkaround video that caught a tech two bays over swearing. Step away from the impact gun, and assume the whole shop is on the recording.
- **Guesses.** Say what you measured and what you saw. "It could be the transmission" is a worry, not a finding.
- **Surprises for the advisor.** If the customer watches the video before the advisor does, the advisor gets blindsided on the callback. The advisor should know what is in it, and should tell the customer at write-up that a video is coming.

## How long should an MPI video be?

About one to two minutes on a car in good shape, three to four when there is a lot to show. Techs who do this well land in the same place: intro, the concern, walk from one end of the car to the other, done. Longer is fine when the car needs it, as long as the customer can skip to the part they care about. Chapters solve that.
