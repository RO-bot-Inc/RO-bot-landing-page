The fixed operations KPIs that matter most fall into four groups: **labor** (effective labor rate, technician productivity and efficiency), **sales** (hours per RO, MPI completion, approval rate), **warranty** (claim rejection and chargeback rates), and **retention** (customer return rate, technician turnover). Track a few from each group every week, and you will see problems while there is still time to fix them in the current month.

Below are 14 KPIs, how to calculate each one, and what moves it.

## Labor KPIs

### 1. Effective labor rate (ELR)

**Formula:** labor sales ÷ hours sold.

ELR shows what you actually collect per hour, after discounts, internal rates, and warranty. It is almost always below your posted door rate. See [Why Your Effective Labor Rate Is Lower Than Your Door Rate](/blog/effective-labor-rate/).

### 2. Technician productivity

**Formula:** hours worked on ROs ÷ hours on the clock.

Measures how much of a technician's paid time is spent on billable work. Low productivity usually points to waiting: for parts, for approvals, for a lift, or for paperwork.

### 3. Technician efficiency

**Formula:** hours flagged ÷ hours actually worked on ROs.

Measures speed against book time.

### 4. Proficiency (overall)

**Formula:** hours flagged ÷ hours on the clock.

Combines productivity and efficiency into one number. Useful at shop level for capacity planning.

## Sales KPIs

### 5. Hours per repair order

**Formula:** total hours sold ÷ number of ROs, tracked separately for customer pay, warranty, and internal.

The clearest signal of whether advisors are presenting additional work. Read [How to Increase Revenue Per Repair Order Without Adding Staff](/blog/increase-revenue-per-repair-order/).

### 6. MPI completion rate

**Formula:** ROs with a completed inspection ÷ eligible ROs.

An inspection that is not done finds nothing to sell. See [Your MPI Completion Rate Is Your Biggest Revenue Leak](/blog/mpi-completion-rate-revenue/).

### 7. Recommended work approval rate

**Formula:** dollars (or lines) approved ÷ dollars (or lines) recommended.

Shows how well findings are presented. Photos and video usually help. See [What Customers Want to See in an Inspection Video](/blog/what-customers-want-inspection-videos/).

### 8. Declined work follow-up rate

**Formula:** declined-work items contacted ÷ declined-work items logged.

Declined work is revenue the customer already knows they need. See [The Cheapest Revenue in Your Store Is Sitting in Declined Work](/blog/recover-declined-service-work/).

### 9. Customer-pay gross profit percentage

**Formula:** customer-pay gross profit ÷ customer-pay sales, separately for labor and parts.

## Warranty KPIs

### 10. Warranty claim rejection rate

**Formula:** claims rejected ÷ claims submitted.

Rejections delay cash and cost administrator time. See [How to Reduce Warranty Claim Denials](/blog/reduce-warranty-claim-denials/).

### 11. Warranty chargebacks (dollars per month)

Track dollars charged back, the reason, and the labor operation. See [Warranty Chargebacks: Why Dealers Lose Money Months Later](/blog/warranty-chargebacks/).

### 12. Warranty labor rate and parts markup vs. retail

If your warranty reimbursement rate is below your customer-pay rate, you may qualify for an increase under your state's law. See [Warranty Labor Rate Increase](/blog/warranty-labor-rate-reimbursement/) and [Warranty Parts Markup Increase](/blog/warranty-parts-markup-increase/).

## Retention KPIs

### 13. Customer retention rate

**Formula:** customers who returned within a set period (often 12 months) ÷ customers served in the prior period. Many stores also track retention of vehicles they sold.

### 14. Technician turnover

**Formula:** technicians who left in the last 12 months ÷ average technician headcount.

Every departure takes capacity and customer relationships with it. See [Your A-Techs Aren't Leaving for a $5k Raise](/blog/automotive-technician-retention/).

## How to use these KPIs

- **Review weekly, not monthly.** A monthly report tells you what already happened.
- **Break them down by person.** Shop averages hide the one advisor or technician driving the result.
- **Pick one or two to improve at a time.** Fourteen goals at once means none get attention.
- **Connect KPIs to causes.** Low productivity, low MPI completion, and short warranty stories often share one cause: documentation takes too long.
