You can add digital vehicle inspections to CDK, Reynolds and Reynolds, Tekion, or most other dealer management systems without replacing the DMS. Most dealerships do it with a separate inspection platform that connects to the DMS, pulls open repair orders and, where the DMS allows it, writes results back. This guide explains how those integrations work, what to check before you buy, and how to roll out inspections without disrupting the shop.

## Why dealers keep the DMS and add a separate inspection tool

Switching DMS platforms is a major project that touches accounting, sales, F&I, parts, and service. Few stores want to take that on to improve inspections. A separate inspection tool lets the service department change how technicians work while the rest of the store keeps its existing system.

The trade-off is integration. A poorly connected tool means double entry: advisors retype findings into the RO, or technicians look up vehicles by hand. A well-connected one feels like part of the DMS.

## How DMS integrations usually work

- **Reading repair orders.** The inspection tool pulls open ROs, so technicians see their assigned vehicles with the VIN, mileage, and customer concern already filled in.
- **Writing results back.** Inspection findings, recommended work, and sometimes repair stories are written back to the RO, so advisors and the warranty administrator work from one record. What can be written back is set by the DMS provider, not only by the inspection vendor.
- **Vehicle history.** Past inspections and declined work are available on the next visit, which helps advisors follow up.

Integration depth varies by DMS and by vendor. Some connections are read-only, some sync in both directions, and some require certification programs with the DMS provider. Ask each vendor exactly what data moves in which direction for your system.

## CDK, Reynolds, and Tekion: what to ask

### CDK Global

Ask whether the vendor connects through CDK's approved integration program, how often data syncs, and whether findings write back to the RO as lines or as notes.

### Reynolds and Reynolds

Reynolds runs its own certification program for third-party vendors. Ask the vendor how they connect, whether it is an approved interface, and whether any additional fees from the DMS side apply.

### Tekion

Tekion is cloud-based and newer to many stores. Ask exactly what the vendor reads from Tekion and what it writes back.

## Rollout checklist

1. **Map today's process.** Who starts the inspection, who reviews it, who quotes it, and how the customer sees it.
2. **Pick a pilot group.** Two or three technicians and one advisor. Include one skeptic.
3. **Set a baseline.** Record current inspection completion rate, red and yellow items found per RO, and approval rate.
4. **Connect the DMS** once the pilot shows the workflow fits.
5. **Train advisors on presenting findings,** not only technicians on capturing them.
6. **Review results weekly** for the first month and compare to the baseline.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Buying on the advisor demo alone. The technician experience decides whether inspections get done.
- Rolling out to the whole shop on day one.
- Not asking what writes back. Where it is limited, decide who enters findings on the RO.
- Not tracking completion rate. Without it you cannot tell whether the tool is being used.
