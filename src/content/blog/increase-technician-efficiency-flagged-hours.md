To raise technician efficiency and flagged hours without burning out your team, remove the non-billable work around each repair rather than pushing techs to work faster. The biggest losses are usually waiting for parts, waiting for approvals, searching for information, and documentation. Fix those, and flagged hours rise while the pace of the actual wrench work stays the same.

## Efficiency, productivity, and flagged hours

- **Flagged hours:** the labor hours a technician is credited for completing.
- **Efficiency:** flagged hours ÷ hours actually worked on ROs. Measures speed against book time.
- **Productivity:** hours worked on ROs ÷ hours on the clock. Measures how much paid time is spent on billable work.

A tech can be highly efficient and still have low productivity if they spend much of the day waiting. In most shops, productivity has more room to improve. See [Fixed Ops KPIs Every Service Director Should Track](/blog/fixed-ops-kpis/) for how these fit with other metrics.

## Where technician time goes

Watch a technician for a full day and log every task that isn't billable. Common entries:

- Walking to the parts counter and waiting.
- Waiting for the advisor to get customer approval.
- Looking up procedures, TSBs, and wiring diagrams.
- Moving vehicles and waiting for a lift.
- Typing inspection results and repair stories.
- Rework from comebacks.

A day-long time study by a shop foreman, done with the technician's knowledge, usually points to two or three causes that account for most of the lost time.

## Eight ways to raise flagged hours

### 1. Stage parts before the job starts

Have parts pull the common parts for scheduled maintenance and known repairs before the vehicle reaches the bay.

### 2. Shorten the approval loop

Send inspection results to the customer with photos or video so they can approve from their phone. Faster approvals mean the tech moves straight to the repair. See [What Customers Actually Want to See in an Inspection Video](/blog/what-customers-want-inspection-videos/).

### 3. Dispatch by skill

Match jobs to technician skill level. A senior tech on oil changes and a junior tech on complex diagnosis both lower efficiency.

### 4. Cut documentation time

Typing inspections and 3C stories can take several minutes per RO. Across a full day that adds up. Voice-first tools let techs speak findings while they work. See [Voice Technology in the Service Bay](/blog/voice-technology-service-bay/).

### 5. Complete every inspection

The MPI is where additional work is found. Each approved item adds flagged hours to an RO the technician already has in the bay. See [Your MPI Completion Rate Is Your Biggest Revenue Leak](/blog/mpi-completion-rate-revenue/).

### 6. Reduce comebacks

Every comeback is unpaid time. Track them by technician and job type, and review the original diagnosis together.

### 7. Invest in tools and information access

Slow scan tools, shared equipment, and shared terminals all cost time. Ask techs what slows them down; they usually know.

### 8. Train on the jobs that pay

Train technicians on the repairs your shop sees most, so the common jobs get faster.

## How to avoid burnout

- **Measure systems, not just people.** If every tech's productivity is low on Mondays, that's a scheduling problem.
- **Share the numbers.** Techs who see their own flagged hours and the causes of lost time can help fix them.
- **Protect diagnostic time.** On flat rate, unpaid diagnosis pushes techs to guess. See [Who Eats Your Warranty Diagnostic Time?](/blog/warranty-diagnostic-time/)
- **Review pay plans.** See [Flat Rate vs. Hourly Technician Pay](/blog/flat-rate-vs-hourly-technician-pay/).
