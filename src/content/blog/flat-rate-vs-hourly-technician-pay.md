Flat rate pay rewards speed: technicians earn the book time for each job, no matter how long it takes, so efficient techs can earn more than the hours they work. Hourly pay rewards time on the clock: technicians earn the same regardless of output, which gives stability but less incentive to finish quickly. Neither plan is better in every shop. Many dealers now use a hybrid, such as a guaranteed base plus flat rate above a threshold, or team-based pay.

This guide compares the main pay plans, what each one does to productivity and retention, and the shop conditions that make or break each one.

## How flat rate pay works

Each labor operation has a published or agreed time. When a technician completes the job, they are paid that time multiplied by their flat rate. If a 2.0-hour job takes 1.5 hours, the technician is paid for 2.0. If it takes 3.0, they are still paid for 2.0.

**Strengths:**

- Directly rewards skill and speed.
- Labor cost tracks labor sales, which protects the store in slow periods.
- Top technicians can earn well above an hourly equivalent.

**Weaknesses:**

- Income swings with car count, parts delays, and job mix. Slow weeks hurt.
- Warranty work is often less attractive because warranty times can be tight.
- Diagnosis and paperwork feel like unpaid time, so techs may rush them.
- Can create competition over the best-paying jobs.

## How hourly pay works

Technicians are paid for hours on the clock, sometimes with a productivity bonus.

**Strengths:**

- Predictable income, which helps with recruiting, especially for newer technicians.
- No penalty for diagnostic time, training, or slow periods.
- Less friction over job dispatch.

**Weaknesses:**

- No built-in reward for productivity, so output depends on management.
- Labor cost stays fixed when car count drops.
- Top performers may leave for flat rate shops where they can earn more.

## Hybrid and alternative plans

### Guaranteed base plus flat rate

Technicians receive a guaranteed minimum (for example, a set number of hours per week) and earn flat rate above it. This protects income in slow weeks and still rewards output.

### Hourly plus productivity bonus

Base hourly pay with a bonus tied to flagged hours, efficiency, or shop gross. Simpler to explain than flat rate, but the bonus needs to be large enough to change behavior.

### Team or pod pay

A small team (often a lead tech, a mid-level tech, and an apprentice) shares the hours the team flags. It encourages mentoring and keeps jobs moving to whoever is free, but needs clear rules for splitting pay.

## Which plan boosts productivity?

Pay plans change incentives, but productivity also depends on what technicians spend their time on. A flat rate technician waiting 20 minutes for a parts run or typing a warranty story is losing money regardless of skill. An hourly technician doing the same is costing the store money.

The biggest productivity gains usually come from removing non-billable work, whatever the pay plan:

- **Parts staging** so parts are ready when the job starts.
- **Fast approvals** so techs aren't waiting on the advisor and customer.
- **Less paperwork.** Inspections and repair stories that take minutes to type cut into every RO. See [Why Auto Techs Hate Paperwork](/blog/why-auto-techs-hate-paperwork/).

## Choosing the right plan for your shop

- **Steady car count, experienced techs:** flat rate or base plus flat rate tends to work well.
- **Recruiting newer technicians:** hourly or a guaranteed base makes the job more attractive.
- **Heavy warranty mix:** consider a guarantee or warranty-specific adjustment so techs don't avoid warranty work.
- **Mentoring is a priority:** team pay rewards senior techs for developing apprentices.

Whatever you choose, explain it clearly, show techs how to track their own hours, and review it with them once a year. Pay is only part of retention. Read [Your A-Techs Aren't Leaving for a $5k Raise](/blog/automotive-technician-retention/) for what else keeps good technicians.

*Pay plans must comply with federal and state wage and hour laws. Check with your HR or legal advisor before changing technician compensation.*
